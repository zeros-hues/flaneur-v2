Prepared local slice, 2026-10-13. The supplied-connection service and proposed SQL are validated inside rollback transactions. They are not connected to application routes, and no permanent schema change has been applied.

`openWalk` validates a canonical owner UUID, explicit IANA timezone, and optional exact context of at most 2,000 characters. The future HTTP layer must derive the owner from its verified session. Callers cannot supply dates, stop membership, completion, or timestamps. Timezone aliases are normalized. The first session fixes that owner's timezone; changing it requires a separate reviewed policy rather than creating extra sessions.

`postgresWalkSessionStore` serializes owner operations with a transaction advisory lock and takes the shared memory-graph lock before reading candidates or checking archive state. The local date and selection reference instant come from the database clock after locking. First creation atomically selects zero to three stops, inserts them in order, seals membership, and completes an empty session. The owner/local-date key is unique. Existing sessions retain their original context and membership on navigation or edited context; completed sessions never refill.

Frozen stops retain historical selection reasons, bounded evidence references with truth layers/provenance, capture/encounter rank inputs, and `contextual-v1` rank version. They do not duplicate notebook or source text. These are records of why a stop was selected, not new Source facts or User confirmations. A future live presentation reader must resolve current evidence and label historical reasons accurately after reviews/revisions.

Selection writes no exposure event. A `shown` event is separate and unique per stop. The live visibility contract remains to be implemented and browser-tested; merely returning a session must not record shown or create a resurfaced Encounter. `opened` requires shown but does not resolve the stop. Explicit kept/useful/reduced/dismissed/passed events resolve it; the session completes only when every frozen stop is resolved. Contact and clipboard outcomes are not accepted as contact events by this service.

Event UUIDs are stable retry identities. A matching committed event replays without resetting history, including after completion or archival. Changed owner/session/stop/kind reuse fails. Fresh events require owner membership, an active canonical target and the current local day; old-day requests expire. No client-supplied occurrence dates are stored. Event `recorded_at` means the database recorded the request, not proof of a physical action at an invented historical time.

On resume, archived or aliased unresolved objects receive a system `unavailable` resolution and are not replaced. They remain in frozen history and must be omitted from active presentation. This records neither shown nor dismissal; all-withdrawn sessions complete without refill. Feedback derives only from that owner's persisted events: shown supplies the exposure timestamp, while reduced/dismissed supplies negative feedback. The prepared selector's seven-day rest and three-dismissal suppression remain initial evaluation constants. Legacy schedules are not silently converted into verified attention history.

Proposed schema: [0016_walk_sessions.sql](../database/proposed/0016_walk_sessions.sql). The three public tables are `walk_session`, `walk_stop`, and `attention_event`. Unique positions/object membership, positions 1–3, immutable sealed membership, terminal completion/resolution, append-only events, and deferred boundary/resolution checks protect the stored session. An unsealed or inconsistent session cannot commit. Terminal event timestamps are copied inside SQL to preserve PostgreSQL precision rather than round-tripping through JavaScript dates.
